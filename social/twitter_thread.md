# Twitter / X Thread — Gap Research

---

**1/ Buy into the gaps?**

A trader I follow made a credit-spread trade on ES last Monday. Took him 5 minutes.

I spent a week trying to figure out if it was a pattern, or just experience.

Here's what surfaced 👇

---

**2/**

I sliced the market 24 different ways: volatility regimes, gap directions, trend contexts.

Most of it was noise.

**Only one regime kept working.**

---

**3/**

When the gap is DOWN and VIX sits in a narrow middle band (15–25), the market quietly walks back toward yesterday's close by 4pm.

7 years. 4 US instruments. Same pattern, same shape, same edge.

Outside that regime? Nothing.

---

**4/**

Calm markets → premium too thin to capture
Panic markets → the tail blows up
Middle markets → consistent edge

The strategy turned out to be less of a trade and more of a **volatility-regime detector**.

When conditions align, take the position. When they don't, sit out.

---

**5/**

The skeptic in me pushed back hard.

→ "You found this by looking. Confirmation bias."

So I trained on 2019–2023 and held out 2024–2026 without peeking.

Out-of-sample, both rules *improved*.

---

**6/**

→ "Sounds great in a model. Real markets eat you with friction."

Subtracted realistic bid-ask slippage from every single trade.

Win rate drops from 97% to 75%. Mean is still solidly positive.

The fragility tests didn't break it.

---

**7/**

One honest casualty:

Russell 2000 (RTY) actively lost money on the held-out 2.5 years.

Dropped from the production universe.

The 4 survivors: ES, NQ, YM — and Nikkei (NKD), which gaps on Asian news and ended up carrying half the yield.

---

**8/**

The most interesting find wasn't the strategy.

It was watching a trader's *intuition* — "sell premium at the open after a moderate gap-up" — turn out to be exact textbook calibration to a real market pattern.

Sometimes intuition is just experience that hasn't been written down yet.
